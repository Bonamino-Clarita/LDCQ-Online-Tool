01

Lucidity is awareness.
Control is what happens next.

A lucid dream is a dream in which you know that you are dreaming. Yet awareness does not automatically mean that you can influence what happens. The LDCQ was developed to assess lucid dream control as a multidimensional ability, rather than a single global skill.

02

Your dream control profile

Seven ways of influencing a lucid dream.

01Own cognition

Maintain lucidity, intentions, focus and stability.

02Own emotion

Regulate fear, excitement and emotional state.

03Own body

Intentionally alter features and sensations of the dream body.

04Own movement

Initiate and control movement within the dream.

05Characters

Influence, summon and interact with dream characters.

06Environment

Change objects, places and the surrounding dream scene.

07Physics

Influence gravity, time and other dream-world constraints.

03

Two complementary scores

Ability and experience are not the same thing.

C

Dream Control Score

How often you successfully achieve a form of dream control when you intentionally attempt it. Scores range from 0 to 1.

E

Dream Engagement Score

How broadly you have engaged with the different control behaviours represented within each domain. Scores range from 0 to 1.

“N.A.” means you have never attempted that form of dream control. It is excluded from the Control Score rather than treated as a failure.

04

Research basis

Developed for research.
Designed to be useful beyond it.

The LDCQ was developed and validated as a 36-item instrument covering seven theoretically driven domains. Validation showed good internal consistency across domains and strong convergent validity with an existing lucid-dream control measure.
